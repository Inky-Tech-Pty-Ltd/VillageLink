# Pathway to Relevance

This document is the evolving response to [Wishlist item #1](../../Wishlist.md#1--pathway-to-relevance). It is not yet a finished pitch. Its job is to preserve the strongest current thinking and evolve toward succinct, compelling answers to the question: **What is the pathway to relevance for Village Link?**

## Current direction

### Identity is a good way in

A family gathering provides an immediately relatable picture of a near-perfect authentication system.

Imagine arriving at **Family Christmas** and opening the front door. There is a microsecond of authentication ceremony. Nobody notices it. Nobody would think to call it authentication. Yet the system is instant, effortless and extraordinarily reliable.

That is a powerful way into the Village Link story because everyone has already experienced the target state. It contrasts sharply with the repeated work, confusion and vulnerability involved in proving identity across isolated digital systems.

The precise story can change — for example, Dad's 90th birthday or Family Christmas — but the important feature is familiarity. The listener should recognise the phenomenon before any technical explanation begins.

### The identity trap

Identity is also a dangerous place to remain.

Village Link can easily be categorised as **another identity project**. The identity field is crowded with sophisticated technologies, standards and competing approaches. Once an audience places Village Link in that category, there is a substantial risk that they stop following the argument upward and instead evaluate it against the identity technologies they already know.

Identity is important, but it is not the scale of the problem Village Link ultimately wants to address.

### The governance fork

The pitch therefore needs an audience-dependent fork.

For some audiences, identity may be the right place to stop. It is concrete, important and immediately comprehensible.

For an audience willing and able to go further, the question becomes:

> **What built the invisible social machinery operating at the family front door, and what problem does it solve?**

Walking through that door does much more than establish identity. A person enters an existing structure of relationships, standing, expectations, permissions, obligations, history and consequences. In other words, they enter a set of **norms and governance**.

Those norms are not isolated. Family norms are layered with and connected to norms maintained by workplaces, clubs, markets, professions, communities, institutions, governments and countless other systems. Their memories may be social or formal, online or offline, encoded precisely or left vague and ambiguous.

Together they form a fabric of enormous complexity and density.

**Village Link gives us a standard way to make threads in that fabric.**

### Why governance exists — and why this is difficult to pitch

The deeper argument begins with cooperation.

An individual organism can obtain resources directly from its environment. Humans, organisations and increasingly machines operate inside collective systems in which resources, opportunities, authority and consequences are distributed through interaction with others.

Individuals have incentives to obtain advantage from those systems. Collectives cannot function if every participant can simply take as much advantage as possible without constraint. Collectives therefore develop constraints: norms, expectations, rules and institutions, supported by memory of behaviour and relationships.

That is governance in a very broad sense.

This argument may be important to the intellectual foundations of Village Link, but it is **not yet a good elevator pitch**. Beginning with competing genes, competing memes, collective action and constraint asks too much abstraction of most audiences. They will glaze over long before the Village Link primitive appears.

## Current communication problem

We need an everyday example of governance that is as immediate and recognisable as Family Christmas is for identity.

The example needs to let the listener experience the phenomenon first and understand the abstraction second.

**Working challenge: FIND THE FAMILY CHRISTMAS OF GOVERNANCE.**

## Pitch architecture under development

```text
Family Christmas
    ↓
Invisible, effortless identity / authentication
    ↓
[STOP HERE for some audiences]
    ↓
"This isn't really about identity"
    ↓
Relatable governance example  ← CURRENT MISSING PIECE
    ↓
Existing fabric of norms + memory systems
    ↓
Village Link provides a standard thread
```

The Boolean fork matters. The objective is not to force every audience through the deepest version of the argument. It is to recognise when identity has done enough work, and when the listener has the interest and conceptual room to move upward into governance.
